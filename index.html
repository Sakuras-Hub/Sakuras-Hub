Piracy 101
People get media three ways. Direct streaming plays in your browser and saves nothing to your device. Torrenting shares files peer to peer, meaning you upload to others while you download. Direct download, DDL for short, pulls files from file hosts. This directory covers all three.

Direct streaming is the simplest. You open a site, press play, and watch. Nothing is saved to your device. The risk is low because the content never touches your disk. This is where beginners should start.

Torrenting is peer to peer. You download pieces of a file from other users while uploading pieces you already have. Your IP address is visible to every peer in the swarm. That is why a VPN is required, not optional, for torrenting.

Direct download pulls whole files from file hosts like Mega or Rapidgator. You get a link, the host serves the file, you save it. No uploading, no swarm. Slower than torrents for popular content, and it works without a VPN for most hosts.

Golden rule one: always run an ad blocker. Install uBlock Origin on every browser you use: ublockorigin.com. Streaming sites and file hosts are covered in ads, and many of those ads carry malware.

Golden rule two: never torrent without a VPN. Your ISP sees your torrent traffic and can send DMCA notices. Peers see your IP. A VPN hides both. There is no exception to this rule.

Golden rule three: never run random .exe files from sketchy sites. Fake players, fake codecs, and fake installers are how malware spreads. If a site tells you to install a player to watch a video, leave the site.

Golden rule four: check that file sizes make sense. A 90 minute 1080p movie is 1.5 to 4 GB. A 700 MB file claiming to be 4K is fake. If the size does not match the content, skip it.

Golden rule five: read the comments on release pages. Users flag broken files, wrong audio tracks, and fake uploads. The comments are the quality control. A release with complaints is a release to avoid.

The 🚫 badge in this directory marks flagged or sketchy mirrors. Avoid those entries. A mirror is a copy of a site hosted on a different domain, used when the original goes down. If a site you use dies, search the directory for its mirror.

Streaming carries lower risk than downloading. You watch in the browser and nothing touches your disk. Downloading from sites with active communities and verified uploads is safer than grabbing random links from strangers.

Use the search bar to filter the directory by category, platform, or tool. Bookmark the pages you trust. Keep your tools updated, because sites change and blockers break.

Start with streaming. It is the fastest way to watch something tonight with the least setup. Add a VPN and a torrent client when you want downloads. Add a debrid service later if you want speed.

Each step builds on the last. Streaming teaches you the sites. Torrenting teaches you the safety habits. Debrid gives you speed. You do not need all of it on day one.

VPNs & Proxies
Your ISP sees everything you do without a VPN, including torrent traffic. That can mean DMCA notices, throttled speeds, or copyright trolls sending threatening letters. A VPN encrypts your connection and hides your IP from peers.

Free VPNs usually cost you in other ways. Many log your traffic or sell your data. The one trusted free option is Riseup, a no-logs VPN run by a collective: riseup.net/en/vpn. It is slow but honest.

Paid VPNs are worth it for torrenting. Mullvad is the standard pick: mullvad.net. No email required, pay with crypto or cash, kill switch built in, WireGuard protocol, and a strict no-logs policy. It costs a flat monthly rate and does not track you.

OpenVPN configs let you use VPN servers with any OpenVPN client. Riseup and Mullvad both publish them. Check that a server is alive before you connect using a service like ipspeed.info.

Proxies are different from VPNs. An HTTP or SOCKS proxy routes your traffic through another server but does not encrypt it. You configure them in your browser or download manager. Free proxies are slow and unreliable, fine for casual browsing, not for downloads. Lists of free proxies live at ipspeed.info.

DNS leaks defeat the purpose of a VPN. If your DNS requests go to your ISP, they can still see what you do. Test for leaks after connecting and switch servers if one leaks.

Bind your torrent client to the VPN interface so it only sends traffic through the tunnel. If the VPN drops, the client stops, not your connection. This is the single most important torrenting safety step.

Privacy, Ad Blockers & Safety
uBlock Origin is the one extension to install on every browser: ublockorigin.com. It blocks ads, trackers, and the popups that streaming sites rely on. Turn on all filter lists in its dashboard for maximum coverage. It is free and open source.

Brave ships with shields built in: brave.com. Shields block ads and trackers without any extension. It is a solid second browser for streaming if you want isolation from your main profile.

Tor Browser is for extreme anonymity: torproject.org. It routes traffic through three relays and hides your identity well. It is slow, so use it for research and sensitive browsing, not for downloads or streaming.

Mullvad pairs with Tor for the strongest setup: mullvad.net. Use Tor for browsing, Mullvad for downloads. Never mix the two.

Never turn off your ad blocker on streaming sites. The popups and redirects they serve carry malware. If a site breaks with the blocker on, find a different site.

Use a password manager and unique passwords for every account. Do not reuse passwords across sites. Do not log into real accounts, email, or banking on sketchy mirrors. Treat every mirror as hostile until proven otherwise.

Keep your browser updated. Most drive-by infections come from outdated browsers and missing blockers. That combination, plus common sense, covers most of the risk.
🚨
Fake Sites & Malware Red Flags
Fake mirrors of real sites are the most common way people get hit. Check the domain before you click. A one-letter-off spelling, a swapped TLD like .top or .xyz, or a hyphen where the real site has none means you are on a honeypot that exists to serve malware.

The fake player is the classic. A site tells you the video needs a special codec or player update, then hands you an .exe. Real streaming sites play in your browser. Anything that asks you to download a player, codec, or stream enhancer is malware delivery.

Popups that demand payment, claim your device is infected, or say your browser is outdated are scareware. Same for fake CAPTCHA walls that tell you to press Win+R and paste a command. No real site needs you to run a command to prove you are human.

A site that asks for your real email, a password, or banking details is not a streaming site. Free sources have no reason to collect that. If a download page asks for a credit card to verify age or unlock the file, close it.

Check reputation before you trust a new domain. r/Piracy keeps a megathread of vetted sites and reviews of new ones. Whois age matters: a site registered three weeks ago on a cheap TLD is a throwaway. Review farms like Trustpilot are gamed, so treat them as weak evidence.

Rule of thumb: if the site fights your ad blocker, demands a login, or pushes a download before showing content, walk away. Sites that tolerate uBlock Origin and serve content without a song and dance are the ones that survive long enough to build trust.

Browser Hardening
Firefox with uBlock Origin is the baseline setup. Install uBlock Origin, leave the default filter lists on, and skip the advanced mode until you know what you are doing. It blocks ads, trackers, and most malicious domains before they load.

Use private windows for streaming sessions. Firefox Multi-Account Containers go further: put sketchy sites in a container that cannot see your cookies or logins from other containers. A streaming mirror never needs access to your main session.

WebRTC leaks your real IP even behind a proxy or VPN. In Firefox, set media.peerconnection.enabled to false in about:config, or force WebRTC through the proxy with an extension. Test at a leak site after you change it.

Turn on resist fingerprinting. Firefox's built-in RFP, privacy.resistFingerprinting in about:config, spoofs your canvas, fonts, and timezone so trackers cannot build a stable ID. It breaks some sites. That is the trade you accept for not being fingerprinted.

Enable HTTPS-only mode in Firefox settings. It upgrades every connection and blocks plain HTTP. Clear cookies after each streaming session and you leave almost no trail on the sites you visit.

Keep a separate browser profile for sketchy browsing. Firefox profiles are isolated: one for your real accounts, one for mirrors and download sites. Never log into your real email or banking from the profile that touches piracy sites. If you prefer downloading over streaming, yt-dlp pulls from hundreds of sites without opening a browser at all.

DNS, Leaks & Ad-Block DNS
DNS is the phonebook that turns a domain name into an IP address. Every site you visit starts with a DNS lookup, and by default your ISP runs that lookup and logs it. Your ISP sees every domain you visit, even when the traffic itself is encrypted.

Encrypted DNS stops that. DoH (DNS over HTTPS) and DoT (DNS over TLS) wrap your lookups so the ISP cannot read them. Firefox and Chrome both support DoH in settings. Windows and Android have system-level toggles.

Ad-blocking DNS filters at the network level. AdGuard DNS blocks ads and trackers for every device on your network, no extension needed. NextDNS gives per-device config, custom blocklists, and logs you can inspect. Quad9 blocks malware and phishing domains with no logging of your IP.

Set it at the router to cover every device, or per-browser if you only care about one machine. Router-level means smart TVs and phones get filtered too. The trade: router DNS applies to everything, so a device that needs unfiltered DNS will fight you.

Test for leaks after you configure anything. dnsleaktest.com shows which DNS servers your traffic actually uses. If you see your ISP's servers, your encrypted DNS or VPN is not being honored and your lookups are still visible.

A VPN that leaks DNS defeats itself. If the tunnel carries your traffic but DNS goes out in the clear, your ISP sees the domains and the VPN is theater. Check IPv6 too: many VPNs filter IPv4 but let IPv6 traffic bypass the tunnel, leaking both your IP and your DNS.

Download Security & Verification
Compare file size against the release page before you download. A 4K movie rip is several gigabytes. A 50 MB file claiming to be the same movie is a trojan wearing a filename.

Verify checksums. Reputable release pages publish SHA-256 hashes. After you download, hash the file and compare. On Windows, Get-FileHash in PowerShell gives you the SHA-256. If the hash does not match the release page, delete the file.

Scan before you run. VirusTotal accepts file uploads up to 650 MB and also accepts a hash, so you can check a file without uploading it. Zero detections is not proof of safety. Several engines flagging a file is a hard no.

Stick to known release groups. Groups with years of history and a consistent naming scheme are the ones that survive. A random uploader with one release and a fresh account is a red flag, no matter how good the title looks.

Password-protected archives are normal in this scene. The password is usually the site name, sometimes in the filename or the release notes. If a file asks for a password and the site does not tell you what it is, that is a setup.

Never run .exe, .bat, .jar, or .msi from an unknown source. Those are the formats that execute code. Video files and images are lower risk, though archives can still hide executables, so extract to a folder and look before you run anything.

For the paranoid, run downloads in Windows Sandbox or a throwaway VM. Open the file there first, then move it to your real machine if it behaves. Fake codecs, fake installers, and fake activation tools are the top three malware disguises in this scene.
Streaming & Watching
📺
Streaming Sites
Free streaming sites are ad supported. Expect popups, redirects, and fake play buttons. Your ad blocker handles most of it. If a site is unusable with a blocker on, move to another site.

Pick sites from the directory that are flagged as active. Check the source list on the entry. When a site is down, use its mirror. The directory lists mirrors next to the main domain.

Browser player tips. Use fullscreen for the best quality. Look for the gear icon to switch quality and subtitles. Some players need a second click on play, or a server switch, before video starts. That is normal.

On mobile, Safari and Chrome handle most players fine. If a player misbehaves, request the desktop site. Some sites serve better players to desktop browsers.

Leave any site that demands payment, a login, or personal details. Legit free streaming never asks for your credit card. If a site asks, it is a scam or a honeypot.

Bookmark the two or three sites that work best for you. The directory changes, and the best sites today may be gone in six months. Keep checking back.
🎬
Movies & TV Shows
Browse the Movies and TV category in the directory, or use the search bar for a specific title. Most free sources carry 720p and 1080p web rips. 4K exists but is rarer and heavier.

Learn to read release tags. A file named with 1080p and WEB-DL came from a streaming service and looks clean. A file tagged CAM or TS was recorded in a theater with a camera. CAM quality is bad, watchable only if nothing else exists. BluRay and REMUX are the best quality, ripped from the disc.

File size tells you a lot. A 90 minute 1080p movie should be roughly 1.5 to 4 GB. A 700 MB file claiming 4K is fake or garbage. If the size does not match the resolution, skip it.

WEB-DL and WEBRip both come from streaming services. WEB-DL is a direct capture, WEBRip is re-encoded. Both are good. BluRay is from the disc, REMUX is the disc stream without re-encoding. REMUX is the best quality you can get.

Check the comments on a release page before downloading. Users flag broken files, wrong audio, and fake uploads. The comments are the quality control.

For streaming instead of downloading, pick a site from the directory and search there. The same release quality rules apply to what the site serves.
🎌
Anime, Manga & Manhwa
Anime streaming sites in the directory update within hours of a Japanese broadcast. Subs are usually available before dubs. If you want the latest episodes, subs are the way.

For downloads, some anime sites let you grab batches with yt-dlp: github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp. A batch download of a full season saves time and lets you watch offline. Check the site's player for a direct video URL first.

Manga and manhwa readers in the directory cover scanlations and official releases. Readers work in the browser. For offline reading, download the chapters as images or CBZ files.

Subtitles for anime come as .srt files. Put the .srt in the same folder as the video and name it exactly like the video file. Most players pick it up automatically. If not, load it manually from the player's subtitle menu.

Fansub groups label their releases with quality tags. Look for 1080p and HEVC in the filename for smaller files with good quality. Older series are often only available in 720p.

Manhwa is Korean webtoon format, read top to bottom. Manga is Japanese, read right to left. The readers in the directory handle both. Set the reading direction in the reader settings.
📚
Books, Audiobooks & Comics
Anna's Archive is the biggest book search engine: annas-archive.org. It indexes millions of books, papers, and comics. Search a title, pick a format, download. That is the whole workflow.

Pick the right format. EPUB for e-readers and phones. MOBI or KFX for Kindles. PDF for tablets, comics, and anything with fixed layout. If a book only comes in one format, convert it.

Calibre manages your library: calibre-ebook.com. It organizes books, converts between formats, and syncs to e-readers. Install it, point it at your downloads folder, and let it sort. It is free and open source.

Audiobooks come as .m4b or .mp3 files. .m4b supports chapters, .mp3 is plain audio. For long books, .m4b is better because you can skip chapters. Play them with any media player.

Comics come as CBR or CBZ files. Those are just zip archives of images with a different extension. Read them with a comic reader app. Calibre can also read them if you prefer one app.

Check the file size before downloading a book. A novel is 1 to 5 MB as EPUB. A 200 MB EPUB is probably a scan or a fake. Comics are bigger, 50 to 500 MB per volume, because they are images.
📡
Live TV & Sports Streaming
Free live-TV sites work differently from on-demand streaming. Most pull channels from public feeds and rotate sources constantly, so a channel that works today may be dead tomorrow. The directory's Live TV category lists the ones that survive longest. Bookmark two or three, not one.

Sports are the hardest case. Big matches draw heavy traffic, and the sites that carry them go down mid-game or get blocked by their own hosts. Expect to switch mirrors. A site that asks for a credit card or an account before showing you a stream is not a free site, close it.

Buffering is normal on these sites, especially during peak hours. Lower the stream quality in the player if you can. A wired connection beats Wi-Fi. An ad blocker is not optional here, these pages load dozens of trackers and pop-under ads. uBlock Origin handles most of it.

Paid IPTV services are a gray area. They resell streams they do not own, they disappear without notice, and you have no recourse when they do. If you pay, treat it as a gamble with no guarantees.

For TV viewing, cast the tab to a Chromecast. The stream plays on the TV while your phone or laptop acts as the remote.

Subtitles Guide
Subtitles come from two reliable places: opensubtitles.com and subdl.com. Both let you search by movie or show name and filter by language. The subtitle file you want matches your video release exactly. If your file is named Movie.2024.1080p.WEB-DL, look for a subtitle with the same tags in its name or description. A subtitle made for a different cut will drift out of sync.

.srt is plain text, works everywhere, and is the safe default. .ass adds styling, fonts, and positioning, which matters for signs and karaoke, but some players render it poorly. When in doubt, take the .srt.

Rename the subtitle file to match the video file exactly, same base name, same folder. Movie.2024.1080p.WEB-DL.srt next to Movie.2024.1080p.WEB-DL.mkv. Most players then load it automatically.

In VLC, drag the subtitle file onto the player window or use the Subtitle menu. In mpv, the same base-name rule means it just works. Both players are free: videolan.org and mpv.io.

Out-of-sync subs are fixable. In VLC, press G and H to shift subtitle timing forward and backward, or use the Subtitle Track Sync menu. In mpv, use the [ and ] keys. Adjust in small steps, 0.5 seconds at a time.

Forced subtitles cover the parts of a movie spoken in another language. They are usually labeled forced in the download. Grab those instead of the full track when you only need the translated dialogue.

The subdl CLI fetches subtitles from the terminal and matches them to your files automatically. It saves the manual search step when you have a folder full of episodes.

Stremio Setup
Stremio is a media player that organizes streaming sources into one interface. It runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS, and most smart TVs. Install it from stremio.com. You log in once and your setup follows you across devices.

The addon system is the core. Community addons add catalogs and sources. Torrentio-style addons pull streams from public torrent trackers and list them next to each title. Addons come and go, the catalog changes, and a dead addon just stops returning results. Reinstall it or pick an alternative.

A debrid account changes the experience. Connect one in the addon settings and streams come from cached files on the debrid service instead of peer-to-peer. Playback starts instantly, no downloading, no seeding, no buffering from slow peers. It is a paid service, but it is the difference between a demo and a working setup.

Pin catalog addons to your home screen so your library is one click away. The trakt.tv addon syncs your watchlist and progress, which matters when you switch between the phone and the TV app.

The home screen is a grid of catalogs you can reorder. Remove the ones you never open and pin the ones you do. The search bar covers everything at once, movies, shows, and the sources attached to each title.

Ad blockers matter less here than on streaming sites, Stremio itself shows no ads. A VPN still matters if you use torrent addons without debrid, because those streams connect you directly to other peers. With debrid, the connection goes to the debrid service instead.

Casting to Your TV
Chromecast is the easiest path from browser to TV. Chrome and Edge have a cast button in the menu, Android and iOS apps have a cast icon inside the player. Tap it, pick the TV, the stream moves over. The phone stops being the source and becomes a remote.

When a site has no cast button, cast the whole tab instead. The browser menu's Cast option has a Sources dropdown, choose Cast tab. The TV mirrors whatever is on screen, including the player controls. Quality drops slightly, but it works on any site.

Stremio and most player apps cast natively. The cast icon appears in the top bar of the player. If your TV runs Android TV or Google TV, install the stremio.com app directly and skip casting entirely.

The directory lists Chromecast, Roku, and Fire Stick as separate categories. Roku and Fire Stick do not support Chromecast casting out of the box. Roku has its own screen mirroring, Fire Stick has Miracast. Check the device's settings before assuming a cast button will appear.

4K casting needs a solid network. The TV and the phone should be on the same band, ideally 5 GHz, and the router should be within range. A weak signal means constant buffering at 4K while 1080p plays fine. Drop the resolution before blaming the site.

No cast support at all? An HDMI cable from a laptop is the most reliable fallback, it never buffers. DLNA apps on the TV can also pull files from a phone or PC on the same network. Android phones without Chromecast can use Miracast, built into most TVs as screen mirroring.

Watchlists & Tracking
Trakt and Simkl are watchlist and tracking services. You mark what you watched, they remember it, and they recommend what to watch next. Both are free with a paid tier that adds nothing essential. Sign up at trakt.tv or simkl.com.

The point is that your history lives outside any single streaming site. Streaming sites change URLs, rebrand, and shut down. Bookmarks die with them. A Trakt or Simkl list survives because it is just data you own.

Mark episodes as you finish them. Both services have a scrobble feature that detects what you are playing and marks it automatically. Stremio has a Trakt addon that syncs progress both ways. Jellyfin and Plex can scrobble to Trakt too, so a show you watch on your media server shows up as watched everywhere else.

Lists are shareable. A public list is a URL you can send to anyone, and they can add it to their own account with one click. Curated lists for genres, years, or moods beat scrolling a catalog.

Tracking matters most when you hop between sites. Watch episode one on one site, episode two on another, and the tracker keeps the thread. Without it you lose track of where you stopped.

Keep your lists portable. Export them periodically, both services support export. If a service changes or dies, your watch history is a file you can import elsewhere.

Downloading Media
yt-dlp is the command line downloader for almost every site: github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp. It pulls videos from hundreds of platforms. Basic use: yt-dlp URL downloads one video. Add --extract-audio to get audio only. Add -f ba for best audio quality. Playlists work with the same command.

Keep yt-dlp updated. Sites change their internals and yt-dlp breaks. Update it regularly or it stops working. The GitHub repo has the latest release.

Video DownloadHelper is a browser extension for streaming sites: Video DownloadHelper. It detects video on the page and offers download options. Good for sites where you cannot find the direct URL.

JDownloader handles bulk downloads from file hosts: jdownloader.org. Copy a link and it grabs everything, including folders of links. It supports dozens of hosts and can run headless with watched folders. The link grabber is the core concept: paste links, it queues them, you start the batch.

XDM accelerates single big files: xtremedownloadmanager.com. It splits the download into segments and grabs them in parallel. Good for one large file from a host that allows it.

Match the tool to the job. yt-dlp for video sites, Video DownloadHelper for browser streams, JDownloader for hoster batches, XDM for single big files. Never download .exe installers from random popups. Get tools from their official sites only.

Torrenting Basics
qBittorrent is the torrent client to use: qbittorrent.org. It is open source, no ads, no bundled junk. Install it, then configure it before you download anything. BitTorrent Classic is the mainstream alternative: bittorrent.com. It works but carries ads.

Bind the client to your VPN before the first torrent. In qBittorrent, set the network interface to the VPN adapter. Enable the kill switch in the VPN app. If the VPN drops, the client stops. This is non-negotiable for torrenting.

Seeding means uploading to other users. Every torrent you download, you also upload. Ratio is upload divided by download. Private trackers require a minimum ratio. Public trackers do not, but seeding keeps the swarm alive.

Private trackers are invite-only communities with strict rules and better releases. Public trackers are open to everyone with looser quality. Start on public trackers, graduate to private if you want.

A .torrent file is a small file describing the content. A magnet link is a URL that does the same thing without the file. Both work in any client. Magnets are easier to share.

Verify file integrity after download. qBittorrent checks hashes automatically. Scan the files with your antivirus before opening. Torrents are the most common malware vector in this space.

A seedbox is a rented remote machine that downloads and uploads for you: seedit4.me. It keeps your IP off the swarm entirely and uploads at datacenter speed. Useful for private trackers where ratio matters.
☁️
Debrid & Premium Hosters
A debrid service is a paid subscription that gives you premium access to file hosts through an API. The service's servers have cached copies of popular files, so downloads are instant, no waiting, no speed limits.

Debrid pairs with JDownloader. Add your debrid account to JDownloader and it downloads through the service's servers. Files that would take hours on a free hoster finish in minutes.

Debrid also pairs with streaming apps. Stremio is the popular one: stremio.com. With a debrid account connected, Stremio streams torrents and hoster files instantly without downloading them to your device. This is the setup most people mean when they talk about streaming everything.

The cache is the key feature. When someone downloads a popular file through the service, it stays on their servers. Everyone after them streams or downloads from that cache. Popular content is instant, obscure content may need to be fetched first.

Cost is a few dollars a month. You bring your own device and your own apps. The service just provides the servers and the API.

This is the advanced tier. Start with streaming, add a VPN and torrents, then add debrid when you want speed and convenience. It is the last upgrade most people make.
🏷️
Private Trackers 101
Private trackers are invite-only torrent communities. You need an account to see the torrent list, and that account is tied to your IP address and your ratio. Public trackers let anyone in; private ones vet every member.

Releases are vetted by uploaders, retention runs for years instead of weeks, and speeds stay high because everyone is expected to seed. The tradeoff is rules: ratio requirements, hit-and-run rules, and bans for cheating your stats.

Invites come from official invite forums, user classes, and recruitment threads on other trackers. Never buy an invite. Paid invites are a scam vector: sellers take your money, the account gets banned, and some trackers ban the whole invite tree.

Every download counts against your ratio. Seed back at least 1:1, or keep torrents alive for the required time. Freeleech torrents do not count against you, grab those first to build buffer.

A seedbox like seedit4.me uploads around the clock, builds ratio fast, and keeps your home IP out of the swarm. Without one, plan downloads around what you can actually seed back.

Watch for IP leaks. If your jurisdiction cares, run a VPN like mullvad.net and bind your torrent client to the VPN interface so nothing leaks. Start on public trackers first, learn the etiquette, then apply for private ones.

Usenet Guide
Usenet is a global message-board protocol that predates the web. Files get split into binary articles and posted to newsgroups. Your newsreader downloads every article and reassembles the original file.

Providers and indexers are separate services. A provider sells access to the servers that store the articles. An indexer searches those articles and returns an NZB file, which lists every article in a post. You need both.

Retention is the selling point. Providers keep articles for years, often a decade or more, so old content stays up long after torrents die.

Usenet is not peer-to-peer. You download straight from the provider's servers at your full line speed. No seeding, no ratio, no leechers. A 50 GB release can finish in minutes.

You need three pieces: a provider account, an indexer account, and a newsreader. The open-source standard is SABnzbd at sabnzbd.org. Install it, enter your provider's server address and credentials, paste in an NZB, and it downloads, verifies, and extracts automatically. nzbget.net is a lighter alternative.

Cost is real. Providers run roughly $5-15 a month. Good indexers charge a one-off fee or a small yearly subscription. Free tiers exist but cap speed, retention, and volume.

Copyright holders send DMCA takedowns and providers remove articles on request. Grab releases in the first days after posting, before takedowns land. Automation helps here.
🤖
*arr Automation Stack
The *arr stack is a set of open-source apps that automate the download pipeline. Sonarr handles TV, Radarr handles movies, Prowlarr manages your indexers, and Bazarr fetches subtitles. All of them coordinate through one download client.

You add a show to Sonarr. It searches your configured indexers, picks the best release, sends it to the download client, waits for completion, renames the file, and moves it into your media library. Bazarr grabs matching subtitles. New episodes download themselves from then on.

The stack needs a download client underneath. qBittorrent at qbittorrent.org is the usual choice for torrents. A media server like Jellyfin or Plex sits on top and serves the library to your TV and phone.

Official docs live at servarr.com. Configuration guides with quality profiles and naming schemes are at trash-guides.info. Expect a real setup session: install each app, wire them together, set up paths, and pick quality profiles.

The software is free. You pay for the hardware it runs on, a PC, NAS, or seedbox, plus whatever your indexers and download client cost. After setup it runs unattended.

This is not a beginner project. You will debug Docker containers, permissions, and path mappings. Start with one app, Sonarr, get it stable, then add the rest.

File Hosts Deep Dive
File hosts store single files and hand them out over HTTP. Free tiers cap download speed, limit concurrent downloads, and daily volume caps. Premium accounts remove those limits.

DDL circles rotate through hosts based on speed and limits. Release pages list the same file on several hosts so you can pick one that works.

Large releases ship as multi-part RARs, usually 1 GB per part. Download every part, then extract part 1 only. One missing part means a corrupt extraction.

Many archives are password-protected. The password sits on the release page, usually in a spoiler tag or a text file next to the links. If the page does not show one, check the site's FAQ.

Some hosts sit behind link lockers: wait timers, captchas, and redirect chains before the real link appears. Skip hosts that make you click through more than a couple of pages.

JDownloader at jdownloader.org is a free link grabber. Paste a page of links and it detects the files, handles captchas, and queues downloads in batches. It also extracts archives after download.

Debrid services act as a middleman: you give them a host link, they download it on their servers, and hand you a fast direct link. That bypasses host limits and speed caps. See the Debrid card.

Check the file size before committing. A 50 GB game split into 50 parts is a long download on a capped free tier. Fake hosts and redirect pages exist to serve malware, stick to the hosts the release page lists.

Android TV Box Setup
The Onn Google TV 4K is the recommended cheap box: onn Google TV 4K. It runs Google TV, handles 4K, and costs about the same as a streaming stick. It is the best value in the category.

Install APKs with a USB drive. Get an OTG adapter: OTG adapter. Plug the USB drive into the adapter, the adapter into the box. Copy the APK pack to the drive first.

Windows Activation
The only recommended method is Microsoft Activation Scripts, MAS: github.com/massgravel/Microsoft-Activation-Scripts. It is open source on GitHub. It activates Windows 10 and 11 permanently with the HWID method, and Office with KMS.

HWID is a one-time digital license tied to your hardware. Activate once, it stays activated through reinstalls and updates. This is the method to use for Windows.

KMS is a 180 day cycle. It activates Office for 180 days and rearms automatically in the background. You never notice the renewal.

Never download random activator.exe files, random KMS zips, or attachments from YouTube videos. Those are how malware spreads. The verified GitHub source is the safe route.

A catbox zip is provided as an alternative: files.catbox.moe/mue96d.zip. It may not work for everything. If it fails, use the GitHub source. The GitHub repo is the authoritative version.

Antivirus may flag activators. That is normal for this category of software. Verify the source is the official repo before running anything. If the file did not come from the listed sources, delete it.

iPhone & iPad Streaming
iOS does not let you install APKs the way Android does. Your options are web apps, streaming sites in Safari, and sideloaded apps that need re-signing to stay installed.

Streaming sites work fine in Safari. Add one to your home screen and it behaves like an app: full screen, its own icon, no address bar. That is the PWA route, and it needs no jailbreak and no computer.

For actual apps, sideloading is the path. AltStore (altstore.io) is the main route: install it on a Mac or Windows PC, connect your iPhone, and it signs apps with your Apple ID. A free Apple ID re-signs every 7 days, so you re-run the process weekly. A paid developer account, or a third-party signing service, removes the weekly refresh.

Region and App Store caveats apply. Some apps and streaming services are geo-restricted, and the App Store shows you only what your region allows. Changing regions is possible but messy with a balance or payment method attached.

Casting to a TV is built in. AirPlay streams from Safari and most video apps to an Apple TV or any AirPlay-compatible TV. No extra hardware needed.

Ad blocking on iOS means Safari content blockers. AdGuard (adguard.com) is the common pick: it filters Safari traffic and system-wide DNS if you enable its profile.

Android boxes remain the easier path for this use case. Sideloading is unrestricted, apps stay installed, and you skip the 7-day signing loop entirely.

Fire Stick Setup
The Fire Stick is a cheap Android TV stick. It runs a locked-down Fire OS, but you can sideload almost anything onto it. The main site is amazon.com.

First, enable unknown sources. Go to Settings, then My Fire TV, then Developer options, then Install unknown apps. Turn it on for the apps you will use. This is the switch that lets sideloaded apps install at all.

Install Downloader from the Amazon Appstore. It is a simple browser that fetches APKs and saves them locally. Use it to grab the APK pack from the directory (files.catbox.moe hosts the current build) and install the APKs inside.

A file manager helps when packs come as folders or when you need to move files around. X-plore and Solid Explorer both run on Fire OS. You mostly need one for clearing leftover files and finding downloads.

When an app breaks, clear its cache first. Settings, Applications, Manage installed applications, then the app, then Clear cache. Most Fire Stick failures are cache bloat, not broken installs.

Storage is tight and Wi-Fi on the 4K stick is mediocre. A USB hub with ethernet fixes both: wired networking for stable 4K streams, and a USB drive for extra space. The stick has no ethernet port, so the hub is the only way.

Fire OS ships with ad rows on the home screen. You cannot remove them without a custom launcher, and launchers reset to the default on reboot. The return-to-home button also goes back to Amazon's screen, not your launcher. Accept the ads or use Wolf Launcher and re-set it after reboots.

Modded & Revanced Apps
Revanced is a patched YouTube client. It removes ads, restores sponsor block, enables background play, and unlocks a few other features. The project lives at revanced.app, with the manager source on github.com/revanced.

The workflow is manager-based. Install Revanced Manager, download the official YouTube APK from a trusted mirror, patch it inside the manager, then install the patched APK. The manager handles version matching and patch selection.

No-root installs need microG. It is a GMS replacement that lets the patched YouTube sign in to your Google account without Google Play Services. Install it before the patched app or login will fail.

Modded music apps follow the same pattern. Community Telegram channels distribute patched Spotify and YouTube Music builds. Those channels are also where scams live: fake APKs, password-protected archives, and files that ask for your Google login. Only take files from channels with a long track record.

Patches break when the upstream app updates. YouTube ships a new version, the patch set lags, and your client stops working or loses features. Expect to re-patch every few months. Check the manager for updated patches before reinstalling.

The safety rule is simple: only use reputable sources. No random APK from an ad, no mirror you found in a comment section, no file that asks for permissions it does not need. The patching route is safer than downloading a pre-patched APK, because you control the input file.

Your Own Media Server
A media server organizes your downloaded library and streams it anywhere. Point it at your downloads folder, it scrapes metadata, and you get a Netflix-style interface.

Jellyfin (jellyfin.org) is free and open source, no accounts, no paid tier. Plex (plex.tv) is the alternative: polished apps everywhere, but the server is hosted and the good features are paid.

Install it on a PC, a NAS, or a seedbox. A desktop PC is the easiest start: run the installer, pick your media folders, let it scan. A NAS runs 24/7. A seedbox runs where your downloads land.

Metadata scraping is automatic. The server matches each file against online databases for posters and cast lists. Name files as Title (Year) and matching is automatic.

Watch on a TV or phone with the official apps. Jellyfin and Plex have apps for Android TV, Apple TV, Fire TV, and phones.

Remote access needs a tunnel or a reverse proxy. Tailscale is the low-effort route: install it on the server and your devices, and your library is reachable anywhere without opening ports.

Pair it with the arr stack and debrid for full automation. Sonarr and Radarr handle downloads, Prowlarr indexes sources, Jellyseerr handles requests. Debrid feeds the stack direct links instead of torrents.

Transcoding is the hardware reality. Direct play means the device decodes the file itself. Transcoding re-encodes on the server and needs CPU or a GPU. A cheap stick direct-plays most 1080p files. 4K HDR is where servers choke.

Kodi Setup
Kodi (kodi.tv) is an open-source media center. It plays local files, runs addons, and turns a cheap TV box into a full player. It runs on Windows, Linux, Android, and most TV boxes.

Install it like any app. On a PC, grab the installer from the official site. On Android, it is on the Play Store and Amazon Appstore. On a TV box, sideload the APK if the store version is missing.

Library mode scans your folders and builds a browsable collection with posters and metadata. File mode just shows the folder tree. Library mode is worth the initial scan.

Streaming addons come and go. Community repos host them, and they break when upstream sites change. Never install an addon from a popup ad or a random YouTube video. Kodi forum repos are the only safe source.

Piracy addons die regularly. Sites shut down, scrapers break, and the addon stops working overnight. Treat any streaming addon as temporary and expect to replace it.

The real use is local playback. Kodi handles CBR and CBZ comics, music libraries, and movie folders better than most dedicated apps. It also works as a frontend for Jellyfin (jellyfin.org) and Plex (plex.tv) via their official plugins.

The default skin is fine. Estuary is fast and complete. Custom skins add looks and lose stability, and most addon guides push skins.

Avoid Kodi builds from random sites. A build is a preconfigured bundle of skin, addons, and settings, and the ones from shady sites are a malware vector. Build your own setup from official addons.

Network-Wide Ad Blocking
Pi-hole (pi-hole.net) and AdGuard Home (github.com/AdguardTeam/AdGuardHome) block ads and trackers at the DNS level. Every device on your network gets filtered, including smart TVs, consoles, and phones.

You need a device that runs 24/7. A Raspberry Pi is the classic choice. An old PC or laptop works. Some routers run either tool directly.

The mechanism is a sinkhole. Your router tells every device to use your Pi-hole or AdGuard Home as the DNS server. When a device asks for an ad domain, the server answers with a dead address and the request goes nowhere.

Setup is a short outline. Install the software, run the setup script, then set the server as the DNS in your router's DHCP settings. Devices pick it up on the next lease. Pi-hole's web interface shows blocked queries live.

The benefits go past ads. Both tools block telemetry domains, malware domains, and known phishing hosts. Fewer requests also means faster page loads.

Caveats exist. Some sites break when their ad domains are blocked, usually blank video players or login loops. The fix is whitelisting the domain in the admin panel. Per-device exceptions need extra config, like separate groups or a second instance.

If you do not want hardware, hosted DNS is the alternative. AdGuard DNS (adguard.com) and NextDNS filter your traffic with no install, just a DNS address change per device. You lose the local dashboard and per-network control.

Music
Soulseek is the peer to peer music network: slsknet.org. It is where rare and niche music lives, and most major releases hit it fast. Search, browse user libraries, download. Files come directly from other users, so quality varies. Check the bitrate before you download.

MP3 vs FLAC. MP3 is compressed, small, plays everywhere. FLAC is lossless, big, sounds identical to the source. For casual listening MP3 at 320kbps is fine. For archiving and good speakers, FLAC.

yt-dlp pulls audio from streaming sites: github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp. Use --extract-audio to get the track. It works for most music platforms and rips any stream to a file.

Tag your files. ID3 tags hold the artist, album, title, and cover art. Untagged files are a mess in any player. Tag them when you download and your library stays clean.

VLC plays everything: videolan.org. mpv is the minimal fast player: mpv.io. Both handle FLAC, MP3, and everything else. Pick one and use it for all audio.

Emulation Deep Dive
RetroArch is the one frontend for most retro systems. It loads emulators as cores, so one install handles NES, SNES, Genesis, PS1, GBA, and DS. Get it from retroarch.com, pull the cores you need from the built-in updater, and setup is done.

Heavy systems get standalone emulators. Dolphin runs GameCube and Wii. PCSX2 runs PS2. Both are mature, free, and updated regularly. Use them instead of RetroArch cores for those systems.

Some cores need BIOS files. PS1, PS2, and DS cores check for system BIOS before boot. The directory's ROM sites carry BIOS packs. Drop them in RetroArch's system folder or the emulator's bios directory and the check passes.

Pull ROMs from the directory's ROM sites. Check file sizes first. A full PS1 game is 300-700MB as .bin/.cue or .chd. GBA games are 8-32MB. A listing that claims a PS1 game is 5MB is a fake or a demo.

.chd compresses disc images into one file. .iso is raw and works everywhere. .zip is fine for cartridge systems. Convert .iso to .chd with chdman to shrink PS1 and PS2 libraries.

Shaders and bezels handle picture quality. CRT shaders emulate scanlines and aperture grille. RetroArch ships dozens built in. A good CRT shader does more for the look than any resolution setting.

Save states let you save anywhere, mid-frame. In-game saves are the game's own system. Use both. Save states for quick sessions, in-game saves for anything you want to keep.

Tune performance: enable frame pacing to stop stutter. Run-ahead cuts input lag by simulating frames ahead. Start with 1 or 2 frames on SNES and Genesis. If a game runs slow, check the core settings before blaming your hardware.

Steam Deck Games
The Steam Deck is a handheld PC. Anything that runs on Linux can run on it. SteamOS ships with a Desktop Mode that gives you a full desktop. Switch to it from the power menu.

Heroic Games Launcher handles Epic and GOG libraries. Install it from the Discover store in Desktop Mode. It logs into both stores and downloads your freebies without the Epic launcher overhead. heroicgameslauncher.github.io

Lutris is the game manager for everything else. It tracks installers, runners, and library entries in one place. lutris.net. Use it for games that do not come from Steam, Epic, or GOG.

Repacks from the directory run on Deck. They are Windows installers, so Proton does the translation. Proton is built into Steam. Add the installer as a non-Steam game, run it once, then point the shortcut at the installed exe. Check the game's ProtonDB rating before committing to a 20GB download.

Emulation on Deck is strong. RetroArch runs great and installs from Discover. Dolphin and PCSX2 have Linux builds that work in Desktop Mode. The Deck handles PS2 and GameCube at full speed.

Storage fills fast. 512GB sounds like a lot until you install three AAA games and a PS2 library. Use a microSD card for ROMs and older games. Buy A2-rated cards. A1 cards are slower and stutter on big reads.

Offline mode works, but plan ahead. Steam needs to verify the device before offline play. Launch each game once while online, then offline mode is reliable. Heroic and Lutris games need their launchers started once online too.

Modern Console Games
The modern console emulator landscape is honest about its limits. Switch emulation took a hit in 2024 when Yuzu and Ryujinx were taken down. Community forks exist. Quality varies by game and by fork. Keep expectations realistic.

PS4 and PS5 have no working emulator. Skip them. Nothing on the directory runs those games on PC.

PS3 is the exception. RPCS3 is real, free, and plays a large chunk of the library. It needs a strong CPU. Most games need 6 cores or more and run better with a dedicated GPU.

Xbox 360 emulation is partial. Xenia runs a few hundred titles, many with glitches. Check the compatibility list before downloading a 7GB ISO.

What to actually use right now: the older generations. PS2, GameCube, Wii, and PSP emulation are mature. Dolphin and PCSX2 run nearly everything at full speed on mid-range hardware. RetroArch covers the rest. retroarch.com

Modern system ROMs and ISOs are large. Switch games run 5-15GB. PS3 games run 5-50GB. Check file sizes before downloading. A 2GB PS3 game is not a PS3 game.

Performance is CPU-heavy. Emulation translates instructions, and that work lands on the processor. A fast CPU with good single-core speed matters more than a big GPU for most systems.

Music Automation
Lidarr is the music member of the *arr family. It works like Sonarr but for albums and singles. Add an artist, and it searches your indexers, sends releases to your download client, and files the results. github.com/Lidarr/Lidarr

Set it up once: point it at your indexers, your download client, and a music folder. Add artists you follow. Lidarr tracks new releases and grabs them automatically.

Soulseek fills the gaps. Lidarr covers mainstream releases through indexers. For rare bootlegs, live recordings, and out-of-print albums, Soulseek is the peer network that has them. slsknet.org. Run both.

Tagging runs on MusicBrainz. Lidarr queries musicbrainz.org for metadata and album art. Keep your tags clean and your library stays searchable. Fix tags before you file the music, not after.

Quality profiles decide what gets grabbed. Set a preferred quality. MP3 320 is the safe default for most libraries. FLAC is lossless and doubles or triples file size. Pick FLAC only if you have the storage.

Automate the library, not the listening. Lidarr handles acquisition and filing. Playlists, mixtapes, and curated folders you build by hand. Automation cannot judge a tracklist.

Lidarr creates a folder structure: Artist/Album/Track. Set the root folder once and it stays consistent. Rename templates keep files uniform.

yt-dlp covers what indexers miss. Soundcloud-only releases, bandcamp exclusives, and live rips. github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp. Run it alongside Lidarr for tracks that never hit a torrent indexer.
